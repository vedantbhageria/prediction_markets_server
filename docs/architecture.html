Prediction markets server

A 24/7 Ubuntu machine that keeps every Kalshi and Polymarket market open now or closed in the last 30 days: Kalshi's one-minute candles, every Polymarket trade and the candles made from them, a search for similar markets across both venues, a viewer and one API.

1The big picture

Two venues in, ten programs, one database, two ways to look at it (check.py, the daily check, reads everything and is left out of the picture).

2The machine and how you reach it

One PC on the office network, reachable only through your own devices.

Ubuntu Server LTS

8 cores, 44 GB RAM, 500 GB NVMe; RAM tested, idle power-saving off.

Tailscale

Private network between server, laptop and iPad; the server is 100.106.150.105.

SSH with keys

Laptop and iPad (Termius) log in with keys, no password needed.

Encrypted DNS

Cloudflare DNS over TLS, because the office network refuses normal lookups.

Public links Funnel

Viewer on https://…ts.net, API on port 8443; each needs its login or key.

Git deploy

Push from the laptop to the server's bare repo and its working copy updates itself.

Network blocks office Wi-Fi

For minutes at a time the network refuses every new connection and name lookup. Connections already open keep working, so the programs keep theirs open (see §8).

No VPN since 3 Nov

Cloudflare WARP was tried for Polymarket and dropped: Polymarket answers directly, and the tunnel broke during every block (see Decisions).

3The programs

Each one starts, stops and restarts on its own; none waits for another. Logs in ~/logs/<program>.log.

discover.py every 15 min

Lists every open market on both venues (each in its own thread), imports newly closed ones, deletes those past 30 days.

activity.py after each scan

Turns each Kalshi scan's volumes into busy-ness scores and picks the hot list (~3 s).

live.py every minute

Kalshi: new candles for hot markets, then any market that traded since.

trades.py every 15 s

Polymarket: reads the feed of every trade on the venue; after a gap, catches up the markets that traded in it.

aggregate.py always

Polymarket: makes one-minute candles from the stored trades, only for minutes that changed.

backfill.py always

History on both venues on leftover budget; --report, --verify, --refetch.

match.py every 10 min

One structured record per market and a vector per question template, for the similar-markets search.

check.py 5 min · daily

A watchdog every 5 minutes that pushes to your phone the moment something needs action, and the full data check every day at 09:00 UTC.

api/ 4 processes

The data API for Python: both venues, one shape (API tab).

viewer.py always

Browser viewer behind a login, light or dark: tables, related wording, nearby markets, charts, manage, /status, /docs (this page).

PostgreSQL with TimescaleDB (candles and trades as hypertables, compressed after 2 days) and pgvector.

5Kalshi: live candles

Spend requests only where trades are happening.

new market score = total volume ÷ minutes since it opened
every scan rate = Δvolume ÷ Δminutes
score = score + (1 − 2−Δminutes/60) × (rate − score) ← one-hour half-life

Trades only

Minutes with quotes but no trade are dropped; nothing is invented.

Late trades caught

A minute is read 60 s after it ends; hot markets re-read the last 2 minutes.

No-change proof

Each reading keeps its ask and answer times, so "unchanged" can never hide a trade.

History

backfill.py goes newest-first back to the open (or 30 days); never-traded markets cost nothing.

6Polymarket: every trade, and candles made from them

Polymarket has no candle API (its price history is a sampled order-book midpoint), so candles are made here from its trades.

Data API v2 since 3 Nov

/v2/trades, taker side only (each fill once). Pages of 1,000 follow a cursor keyed on (time, sequence): pages never overlap or leave holes, and there is no depth limit.

The feed

Every 15 s, the whole venue's trades back to where the last read reached: about 1,000 trades a minute, one or two requests. Coverage is claimed up to 30 s before its newest trade.

Gaps

One feed read goes back at most 100,000 trades (~1.5 h). Past that a gap is recorded; markets whose volume rose in it are read one by one.

History in batches

A market's whole list, newest first, stored and its coverage recorded every 50,000 trades: any market is read in full, and a failure keeps what was stored.

Separate aggregator

Writers mark the minutes they add trades to; aggregate.py claims marked minutes first, then reads their trades, so it never misses one committed mid-way.

Same ids as v1

Trades read through v1 and v2 hash to the same id (checked one by one on stored markets), so the switch stored nothing twice.

7Staying inside the limits

One bucket of request tokens per venue in Redis; every program draws from it, by priority.

Kalshi: 240 a minute

No 60-second window can exceed it however many programs run; at most 15 a second. Hot list size follows the budget.

Polymarket: 300 a minute

Its own bucket, same rules, at most 8 a second. Polymarket allows 300 per 10 s on /v2/trades, so there is room to raise it.

429 halves it

A throttle from either venue halves that venue's budget for every program at once; it recovers slowly.

Priorities

hot (live, the feed) → warm (catch-up, pushed backfills) → backfill, on what is left.

8Similar markets

Every market becomes the same record; a vector finds the nearest wording, the fields measure the rest. Nothing is stored about pairs: a search runs when asked.

StepWhat happens
Recordpropositions.py reads each market: native fields first (Kalshi's strike type, floor and cap, outcome names; Polymarket's group item), regex for the rest (comparators, numbers like $70k or 300-400k, dates, placeholders).
MaskNumbers, dates, comparators and the named outcome become slots: "Bitcoin above ⟨x⟩ on ⟨date⟩". A whole price ladder shares one template.
EmbedEach distinct template once, with bge-small-en-v1.5 (384 numbers) on the CPU; new templates every 10 minutes, open markets first.
SearchThe template's nearest neighbours by cosine distance from the HNSW index of each slice asked for (venue × open/closed), then every market behind them.
Measuretext (cosine), days between the dates the questions turn on, level (relative gap between thresholds), opposite (above vs below).
Labelrelate.py reads the pair: identical, complement, nested, overlapping, same_outcome or related. A reading aid; it filters nothing.
distance = text + 0.10 × min(days, 730) / 365 + 0.50 × min(level, 1) + 0.05 × opposite
limits: max_cosine (default 0.3), max_days, max_level · gates: subject, keywords

Why embeddings

"BTC" and "Bitcoin", "Fed cuts" and "FOMC lowers" are far apart in letters and close in meaning.

Why not only embeddings

They score "above $70k" and "below $70k" about 0.98 alike, so comparators and numbers are kept out of the vector and measured from the fields.

No language model, no training

Regex reads ~98% of real Polymarket questions; an off-the-shelf model is enough until thousands of confirmed matches exist to learn from.

check.py, at 09:00 UTC (the quietest hour on both venues), over the day before. Each check is ok, warn or fail.

10When things go wrong

Everything is saved as it arrives, and bookkeeping only moves once data is written: nothing lost, nothing fetched twice.

What happensWhat the system does
A program crashesRestarts and carries on from the bookkeeping.
Network refuses new connectionsPrograms keep working on connections already open; requests that need a new one wait, one check every 30 s.
Polymarket feed falls behindThe gap is recorded; markets that traded in it are caught up one by one; the feed starts again from now.
A venue changes its APIRequests answer 4xx and the log says so; the daily check fails on any 410, any deprecation notice and any trade missing a field. Polymarket retired v1 on 2 Nov and it went unnoticed for 6 hours.
Two writers on the same rowsEvery writer locks bookkeeping rows in key order, so they queue instead of deadlocking.
A venue throttlesThat venue's budget halves for everyone at once, then recovers slowly.
Discovery stops mid-scanSaves its place after every page and finishes the same scan from there.
Redis is downEach program falls back to a third of the budget; live works out the hot list itself.
Server off for daysDiscovery re-imports closed markets; live and the feed catch up; backfill fills the rest.
